Core Formulation

Research Aim & Five Operational Objectives

Bridging psychological needs elicitation to front-end software engineering.

🎯 PRINCIPAL RESEARCH AIM

To establish the educational needs and interface design requirements of autistic learners through a user-centered, stakeholder-informed needs assessment, and subsequently to develop an autism-adapted web-based educational interface that translates these requirements into practice.

1️⃣ Needs & Profile Identification

Identify educational needs, cognitive characteristics, and digital barriers via 30 teachers, 10 therapists, and 10 supported students.

2️⃣ UI/UX Feature Importance Ratings

Quantify stakeholder valuation across 18 interface requirements and compare perspectives between teachers and therapists.

3️⃣ Triangulated Requirements Matrix

Synthesize rating scales, 118-item DP-TIC-AUT-Q scores, and qualitative interview themes into High and Medium priorities.

4️⃣ Interface Operationalization

Build "Learn & Grow"—an 11-screen accessible, single-page application incorporating sensory tranquility and concrete learning.

5️⃣ Evidential Traceability: Document an explicit audit trail proving how every color, button, sound, and layout traces directly back to empirical data and psychological theory.

Theoretical Foundations

The Tripartite Theoretical Framework

How psychological learning theories govern interface architecture.

🧠 Cognitive Load Theory (CLT)

Sweller (1988); Sweller et al. (2019)

  • Working Memory Bottleneck: Autistic executive functioning differences amplify extraneous load.
  • Clutter Elimination: Moving banners and ads deplete processing capacity needed for learning.
  • Singular Focus: Presenting one task card per screen (Step 1 of 5) frees cognitive bandwidth.

🌐 Universal Design for Learning (UDL)

CAST (2018); Meyer et al. (2014)

  • Multiple Representations: Dual coding with paired SVG symbols and optional speech read-aloud.
  • Multiple Action Modes: Large touch targets (≥48px) and tap-to-count manipulatives.
  • Flexible Engagement: Unhurried self-pacing with zero anxiety-inducing timers.

🧩 TEACCH Structured Teaching

Mesibov et al. (2004); ISO 9241-210

  • Predictable Boundaries: Locked Back and Home anchors in identical coordinates on all screens.
  • Explicit Completion: Visual progress steps and completion stars to relieve anxiety.
  • Human-Centered Iteration: Multi-stage feedback cycles from educators and clinicians.
Literature Review

Autistic Cognitive & Sensory Profiles

Neurodevelopmental characteristics and their direct interface design implications.

👁️ Perceptual Processing & Weak Coherence

Literature: Mottron et al. (2006); Happé & Frith (2006); Keehn et al. (2013)

Autistic perception exhibits enhanced low-level processing and detail-focused attention. Peripheral graphics or animations strongly compete for focus.

→ Design Mandate: Remove all advertising banners, sidebars, and autoplay animations.

⚡ Executive Functioning & Memory

Literature: Demetriou et al. (2018); Boucher et al. (2012)

Moderate difficulties across planning, working memory, and mental flexibility. Multi-step activities without cues cause frustration and abandonment.

→ Design Mandate: Chunk learning into step meters (Step 1 of 5) with visible completion cues.

🔊 Sensory Reactivity & Auditory Modulation

Literature: Ben-Sasson et al. (2009); Robertson & Baron-Cohen (2017)

Atypical sensory modulation in >90% of individuals. Stark white screens cause glare fatigue; sudden loud buzzers trigger acute sensory defensive withdrawal.

→ Design Mandate: Warm ivory canvas (#FAF8F5) and pure sine-wave gentle chimes (440–659 Hz).

🖐️ Fine Motor Control & Dyspraxia

Literature: Fournier et al. (2010); Pavlov (2014)

Significant motor coordination differences. Precise clicking on small desktop hyperlinks is extremely error-prone on touchscreens.

→ Design Mandate: All interactive targets exceed 48×48 px with generous touch padding.

Empirical Results

Six Critical Digital Barriers in Mainstream Tools

Reported by N = 40 professionals during semi-structured qualitative interviews.

Moving Banners & Ads

95.0%

Reported by 38/40 professionals (29 Teachers, 9 Therapists). Involuntarily grabs visual attention, causes accidental clicks, and derails the lesson.

Sudden Loud Audio & Fanfares

92.5%

Reported by 37/40 professionals (100% of Therapists!). Causes startle responses, ear-covering, and immediate closing of the browser tab.

Harsh Error Buzzers & Red Alerts

87.5%

Reported by 35/40 professionals. Triggers acute emotional frustration, feeling "attacked," and total task refusal.

Shifting Navigation / Moving Buttons

85.0%

Reported by 34/40 professionals. Moving the Back button induces spatial panic and creates total adult dependency.

Split Layouts & Multi-Step Prompts

87.5%

Reported by 35/40 professionals. Overwhelms working memory; children cannot initiate tasks when two things are asked at once.

Stark White Backgrounds & Glare

75.0%

Reported by 30/40 professionals (90% of Therapists). Causes severe photophobic visual fatigue and reduces on-task stamina.

Discussion

Discussion: Reframing Autism Interface Design

Challenging mainstream gamification orthodoxy through empirical evidence.

🕊️ Calm Over Stimulation

Mainstream software equates engagement with flashy animations and loud celebratory fanfares. For autistic learners, this induces sensory fragmentation and flight-or-fight defense.

Finding: Visual calm and gentle sine tones foster genuine sustained cognitive engagement.

🧭 Navigation as Emotional Security

Near-unanimous agreement on consistent layout (REQ01, 4.78) and permanent Back buttons (REQ05, 4.78) shows that spatial predictability is not merely convenient—it provides affective security.

Finding: Stable navigation relieves anxiety, freeing working memory for curriculum mastery.

🇮🇳 Policy Alignment (India)

Directly operationalizes the Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act (RPwD, 2016) and National Education Policy (NEP 2020) mandates for accessible digital learning resources.

Finding: Open-access, zero-cost web software eliminates institutional budget barriers.

💡 Theoretical Summary: Autism-adapted design is not about childish graphics—it is about cognitive clarity, sensory dignity, and emotional predictability.
